# Using our pilots on the website

Read this before adding a customer, a number or a pilot mention to any page.

## What we have

| Customer | How we may describe it | What we may say |
|---|---|---|
| Sports electronics manufacturer | "a sports electronics manufacturer" (Evan, Nov 2026) | A Decision Model for truck planning: no overstock or stockout, fewer truck rolls than before. Described in words. |
| Aircraft fabrics manufacturer | "an aircraft fabrics manufacturer" (Evan, Nov 2026) | A Decision Model was built for their production-planning use case. No numbers: there are no real results to claim. |

Never name either company. Dr. Hareesh decided the aircraft fabrics work shows no metrics, including the ERP match rate that used to be on the Results page. Do not mention ERP data problems.

Do not put a metric for one customer beside words for the other. On the Results page both are explained the same way, in words. Use a metric only on its own, where it supports a point about the company's work, and only with its exact definition confirmed.

Do not call either one "in progress", and do not state how many pilots we have run.

## The rule: one place for detail, light touches elsewhere

Showing the same pilot everywhere makes it look thin. Each place has one job.

| Page | Job | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Results | The only page with detail | Two customer cards in the same format, in words, no numbers. Then what a team gets, the evidence labels and the fit checklist. |
| Home | One proof line | "Used in pilots with a sports electronics and an aircraft fabrics manufacturer." The scorecard links to Results. |
| Production commitment | Context for one decision | Piloted with an aircraft fabrics manufacturer. |
| Solutions | A tag, no numbers | "Production commitment" is tagged with the aircraft fabrics pilot, "Inventory and allocation" with the sports electronics pilot. |
| Company | Traction for investors | One fact: pilots in sports electronics and aircraft fabrics manufacturing. |
| Product, Resources | Nothing for now | Add a case-study article to Resources only when both sides approve it. |

No metric is shown today. When one is added, define it exactly first.

## Claims rules

1. Every number has an owner, a source and an approval from Evan and Dr. Hareesh before it is public.
2. A customer with no measured result shows the use case that was built, never a number.
3. Label every result: Measured, Demonstrated, Customer-validated or Modeled. No result is claimed today.
4. Describe the customer by industry and the kind of decision. Do not add plant, material or machine details that could identify them.
5. Say "one pilot, not universal" beside the number.

## Why this approach

Practitioner guidance on anonymous proof says the same thing: describe the shape of the client, state the scope, frame early numbers as directional, get sign-off before publishing, and do not publish results for a pilot that has not concluded. Named customers are more convincing, so ask for permission to name them once a pilot is complete.
