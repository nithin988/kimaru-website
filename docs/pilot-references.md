# Using our two pilots on the website

Read this before adding a customer, a number or a pilot mention to any page.

## What we have

| Pilot | How we may describe it | Status | What we may say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sports electronics manufacturer | "a sports electronics manufacturer" (Evan, Nov 2026) | Measured, real data | New-order on-time production rose from 43% to 75%. One pilot, not universal. |
| Aircraft fabrics manufacturer | "an aircraft fabrics manufacturer" (Evan, Nov 2026) | In progress, no real results | The kind of decision (production planning) and that Kimaru is being applied to it. No numbers. |

Never name either company. Dr. Hareesh decided the aircraft fabrics pilot shows no metrics, including the ERP match rate that used to be on the Results page. Do not mention ERP data problems.

## The rule: one place for detail, light touches elsewhere

Showing the same pilot everywhere makes it look thin. Each place has one job.

| Page | Job | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Results | The only page with detail | Two pilot cards: one measured (with the 43% to 75% chart), one in progress (no number). Then what a team gets, the evidence labels and the fit checklist. |
| Home | One proof line | The hero line: "In a pilot with a sports electronics manufacturer, on-time production went from 43% to 75%." The scorecard links to Results and no longer repeats the number. |
| Production commitment | Context for one decision | The same hero line, because this is the decision the pilots address. |
| Solutions | A tag, no numbers | The "Production commitment" decision area says it was piloted with both manufacturers. |
| Company | Traction for investors | One fact: two manufacturing pilots, sports electronics and aircraft fabrics. |
| Product, Resources | Nothing for now | Add a case-study article to Resources only when both sides approve it. |

The 43% to 75% figure appears on three pages: Home (hero), Production commitment (hero) and Results (full). Do not add it anywhere else.

## Claims rules

1. Every number has an owner, a source and an approval from Evan and Dr. Hareesh before it is public.
2. A pilot that is not finished shows its status and the decision, never a result.
3. Label every result: Measured, Demonstrated, Customer-validated or Modeled. Only "Measured" is claimed today, for one pilot.
4. Describe the customer by industry and the kind of decision. Do not add plant, material or machine details that could identify them.
5. Say "one pilot, not universal" beside the number.

## Why this approach

Practitioner guidance on anonymous proof says the same thing: describe the shape of the client, state the scope, frame early numbers as directional, get sign-off before publishing, and do not publish results for a pilot that has not concluded. Named customers are more convincing, so ask for permission to name them once a pilot is complete.
